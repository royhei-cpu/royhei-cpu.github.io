STEP-BY-STEP REFERENCE

Use the website at your own pace.

01. Browse. Compare. Develop.

Homecare now brings market research and product development into one workspace. This guide covers the current website: browsing products, comparing national brands, and building a white-label proposal.

Open this section ↗
02. Find the right section.

Start with the main navigation. Browse competitors, Global retailers and items, Sales and analysis, and White Label Workshop are the main working sections. Brands and factories and Video guide are resources. Opportunities comes last.

Open this section ↗
03. Find international products.

To find international products, open Global retailers and items. Choose a Country or market, then a Retailer, and use Find an item to search. For China, use the China listings shortcut. Open Source listing to check the retailer's own page.

Open this section ↗
04. Keep a useful shortlist.

Use Select to compare on a global product card, or Select in the original catalog. Compare selected opens your shortlist. Export selected CSV downloads the selected records for Excel. Product photos are exported as links, rather than embedded pictures.

Open this section ↗
05. Read the evidence carefully.

Sales and analysis shows the saved evidence. Compare sales figures only when the retailer, metric, reporting period, and snapshot date match. Missing sales information stays unknown. Research coverage is not market share.

Open this section ↗
06. Home care, beyond cleaning.

White Label Workshop opens a dedicated workshop page. Choose a Home-care subcategory and a Product concept. Explore laundry, storage, waste, odor control, and accessories. Follow the three review steps: Real products, Why and what changes, then Design and costs.

Open this section ↗
07. Compare real brand products.

Start with National-brand photo references. The laundry sorter uses Joseph Joseph and Brabantia. Choose View full-size photo to inspect the image. Read its match note, Size and capacity, Manufacturer data, and Photo source before comparing.

Open this section ↗
08. Make the difference specific.

Next, open Why and what changes. Read Why consider white label, then expand Brand reference and proof needed. Suggested changes also compares existing features, proposed improvements, and validation. These ideas still need a Target assortment check.

Open this section ↗
09. Review dimensions and finishes.

Continue to Design and costs for the proposed Blender concept. Use Specs and dimensions to switch between millimetres and inches. Choose a finish to update the interactive model. Use Studio, Front, or Exploded. The rebuilt laundry sorter also has Inside and Details views.

Choose Rotate in 3D for the interactive model. Finishes color the frame and lid; canvas and webbing retain natural tones. A1 is a visual revision; A0 engineering targets still need validation.

Open this section ↗
10. Build a cost scenario.

BOM and pricing lets you adjust component costs and assumptions. Enter a duty rate before calculating landed cost. Costs and proposed retail prices are planning estimates, not supplier quotes.

Open this section ↗
11. Save a complete development brief.

Download the Blender development pack for the base Sage model, renders, dimensions, specifications, and BOM. Save brief with comparison photos keeps your selected finish and pricing edits in an HTML brief. Export references and changes saves a source-linked CSV. Review Validation before proceeding.

The laundry-sorter A1 development pack includes five renders and the national-brand reference photos, alongside the model and A0 dimensions, specifications and BOM.

Open this section ↗
12. Investigate opportunities last.

Use Opportunities last to investigate possible gaps after reviewing the products and evidence. Music and button sounds have separate controls. You can keep the workspace quiet while you browse, compare, and develop.
